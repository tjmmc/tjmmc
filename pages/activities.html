Activities

tjm².

Our own 8-hour math modeling hackathon — free food, $2500 in prizes, and no experience needed. Runs mid-October to mid-November.

Everything About TJM²

competitions.

We compete in the M3 Challenge, HiMCM, SIMIODE, and the Modeling the Future Challenge. The M3 Challenge is the big one: a national competition where teams get 14 hours over Challenge weekend to write a solution paper on a real-world problem unknown until they log in — building models, justifying assumptions, defining parameters, and analyzing effects of change. The top 6 teams receive tuition scholarships and present their papers to professionals in New York City (the trip is paid for). We've recently won $20,000 and $15,000.

8th periods & discord.

Lectures on math modeling to prepare for the contests — MATLAB, regressions, paper-writing, and more. Browse the archive on the lectures page. Have an idea or want to present? Pitch us at {{ site.data.links.email }}, or hop in our discord.

research projects.

Group-wide or club-wide research projects, either academic or serving the nearby community — always with an external deadline: someone outside waiting for the result, or a presentation date on the calendar. We self-publish the best papers, project reports, and competition submissions, and build usable public tools on our GitHub.

in-house contests.

Periodic internal contests that feed into team selection — like predicting the future a month out, so there's suspense when the answer arrives. Plus our problem of the month. Compete for prizes and food.

talks.

We bring in experts to show how math modeling shows up in day-to-day jobs — rockets, weather, materials, medicine, finance, and more — with Q&A. Talks can run during 8th period and stream to younger students at local middle schools. Each speaker can become a future judge or a project mentor.

practice & games.

Fermithons. The Metaculus forecasting competition, with its own small monetary prize (~$100 per winner) and fall and winter rounds. A year-long internal Brier score leaderboard — and maybe even play-money prediction markets on school events.
